Carousel Types

Carousels do the heaviest educational lifting — they hold attention and get saved, which social platforms reward.

Explainer

How it works

Step-by-step: choose your plan → we cook → we deliver → you eat. Removes confusion at the top of the funnel.

Myth-busting

Healthy ≠ boring

Pairs a common belief with a real Harvest Box meal that disproves it. Highly shareable.

Nutrition

Inside the meal

Macro/calorie breakdown of one dish per slide. Builds credibility with health-conscious buyers.

Social proof

Real customer quotes

One testimonial per slide with a photo. Trust-building, low production cost.

Comparison

Cook vs. subscribe

Time, cost and effort side by side. Makes the value proposition concrete.

Offer

This week's menu

Visual menu preview to build anticipation before each weekly cycle.

Video / Reel Types

Short-form video drives reach. Every reel should hook in the first 1.5 seconds and end on the delivery box or CTA.

Unboxing

Doorstep to open-box in 10–15 seconds. Satisfying, high completion rate.

Unscripted 20–30 second reaction. The single highest-converting format.

POV

Follows one person's day, meal by meal. Highly relatable format.

Trend audio

Meal reveal to trending sound

Rides platform trends for extra reach at zero cost.

05

What Works For You — And Against You

An honest read before we spend a single dollar on ads.

✓ In your favor

  • Recurring revenue — subscriptions mean predictable income, not one-off sales.
  • Health trend is rising — demand for convenient healthy eating keeps growing.
  • Visual product — food photographs and films naturally well for social.
  • Low-friction retention — once someone subscribes, habit does the retention work.
  • Word of mouth potential — satisfied subscribers share meals organically.

⚠ Watch out for

  • Delivery is the weak link — one late or cold delivery can lose a subscriber for good.
  • Price sensitivity — subscriptions compete with "I'll just cook it myself."
  • Menu fatigue — repeating meals too often drives cancellations after week 3–4.
  • Trust barrier — new customers hesitate to commit to a subscription, not a single order.
  • Small budget — $100 buys attention, not scale; targeting has to be precise.
06

$100 Sponsoring Budget

Small budget, so it goes toward proven creative only — never toward untested content. Split across two platforms and one retargeting pass.

Instagram & Facebook boosted posts (Meta Ads)$45
TikTok Spark Ads on best-performing reel$30
Retargeting — page visitors & profile engagers$15
Buffer for the single best-converting ad, week 4$10
Total$100.00

How we spend it

  • Boost only content that already has organic proof (saves, shares, comments) — never a cold post.
  • Run the reel testimonial or unboxing video first; those formats historically get the lowest cost-per-result in this category.
  • Keep radius targeting tight to your actual delivery zone — every dollar outside it is wasted.
  • Reserve $10 to double down on whichever ad gets the cheapest cost-per-subscription click by week 3.
07

How We Follow the Strategy

A plan is only as good as the review habit around it. Here's the weekly rhythm.

Daily posting log

Every post logged with format, topic and publish time so nothing in the roadmap gets skipped.

48-hour performance check

Reach, saves, comments and link clicks checked 48h after each post — enough time for the algorithm to settle.

Weekly review call

Every Sunday: compare the week's actual numbers to the goal, agree what to repeat and what to drop.

Ad spend check-in

Every 3 days during any boosted campaign — pause underperforming ads before the budget drains.

Month-end report

A one-page summary vs. the goals below, plus next month's plan adjusted on what actually worked.

09

If We Don't Reach the Goal

Every goal gets a fallback decided in advance, so we react with a plan — not in a panic.

Trigger: followers under target by week 3

Shift budget to reach

Move the $10 buffer into broader reach ads instead of retargeting, and increase story frequency to 2x/day.

Trigger: clicks high, subscriptions low

Fix the offer, not the ads

It's a landing page or pricing problem, not a traffic problem. Add a first-week discount and simplify checkout.

Trigger: engagement rate below 3%

Double down on testimonials

Testimonial and behind-the-scenes formats consistently outperform polished ads — shift the mix toward them.

Trigger: ad cost-per-click too high

Narrow the targeting

Cut the delivery radius further and target only warm audiences: page visitors, followers, lookalikes.

Trigger: subscribers churn after week 1

Fix the menu, not the marketing

Survey cancelling customers directly — usually a variety or portion issue the kitchen needs to solve.

Trigger: overall goal missed at month end

Extend, don't panic

Roll the remaining budget and best-performing content into a focused 2-week push instead of starting over.
